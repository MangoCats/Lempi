Advanced Features

Everything below exists somewhere in Lempi or Vipunen today. Quite a lot of it — settings, Bluetooth, play history, the full "why did it pick this" breakdown — only exists in the Lempi skin; the table at the bottom of this page says exactly what's where.

Settings (Lempi skin only)

The gear icon opens a settings panel with a run of fields that shape playback behavior:

  • Skip fade — how long a manual skip fades out over, rather than cutting instantly.
  • Next starts at — how much lead-in time the next track gets before the current one actually ends.
  • Save position every… — how often the current playback position is written down, so a restart resumes close to where it left off.
  • Hold a skipped/removed track out for… — two separate windows: how long a track you skip, or a track you remove from the queue, is held back from being offered again. This is distinct from the Cooldown dial in Preference Tuning, and shorter.
  • Keep queued ahead — how many tracks Lempi tries to keep lined up in the queue at once.
  • Speaker delay, Follow another player and Get in step — for playing the same programme on two or more players at once, in step: this player's own delay in milliseconds, the name of the player to follow (empty means play this player's own programme), and whether to fall into step straight away (cutting what is playing, the way a skip does) or at the other player's next track. The panel explains the trade-off, and that being exactly in step needs both players' clocks set by the same time server.
  • Output volume — a volume level remembered for each output, the DAC or a Bluetooth speaker: Remember pins the current level for the output in use, Clear forgets it. Bluetooth timeout is how long to wait for a Bluetooth speaker before falling back to the DAC.
  • Guest sampling interval — only relevant if another playback backend (like MPD) is attached alongside Lempi.
  • Playing through — which backend is actually driving audio right now, if more than one is available; hidden entirely when there's only the one.
  • Status LED — on a Raspberry Pi appliance, controls the small light on the device itself. Four choices: Always on (the default, with its own brightness slider), Live Wi-Fi indicator (the light reflects whether Wi-Fi is currently switched on — useful on an appliance, since it's the only sign of life if the network drops), Always off, or Leave to the system (Lempi doesn't manage it at all). Below full brightness, "Always on" blinks very fast to look dimmer rather than glowing at reduced power — this LED has no true hardware dimmer. Has no effect on playback either way, and the choice is remembered and reapplied automatically at every restart, including a full power cycle.
  • Five file-writing toggles, each with its own on-screen explanation: naming capture tracks for other clients (cue sheets), saving album art for captures, keeping per-song lyrics for a local client, keeping lyrics files beside the audio itself, and keeping a lossless FLAC copy of each CD rip Vipunen adds. Some of these only take effect after a restart — the panel says which.

The same panel also shows the exact build you're running (with a copy-to-clipboard button), which branch and commit it came from, and whether the working copy has uncommitted changes — useful when reporting a problem or checking whether an update actually landed.

Bluetooth and other radios (Lempi skin only)

The same settings panel lists every radio the device has (Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, mobile) with on/off switches — a radio carrying your only route to this page can't be switched off from here, for the obvious reason. The speaker chooser scans for, pairs with, connects to ("use this one"), or forgets a Bluetooth speaker. After connecting, a "can you hear it?" check gives you thirty seconds to confirm before automatically reverting if you don't — so a bad connection can't strand you silently. On a Raspberry Pi appliance the speaker's own buttons work too: play/pause and skip on the speaker control Lempi directly. The previous-track button does nothing, except while the appliance is running its own Lempi network, when it means “I'm home” — see Coming home below. Which buttons a speaker actually sends differs from model to model; volume is always the speaker's own.

Travel, offline playback, and automatic Wi-Fi failover (Lempi skin only, Raspberry Pi appliance only)

When you take a Pi appliance travelling or move it out of range of its home network, you don't need a screen, keyboard, or pre-configured router: it takes care of itself. If it boots where no known Wi-Fi is reachable (or loses connection for 30 continuous seconds), it automatically starts its own access point named Lempi (default password: Lempi321).

First-class offline playback: You can join that network from your phone, tablet, or laptop and reach the player directly at plain http://lempi/ or http://10.42.0.1/ — no port number needed (port 5720 works as well). An Offline Player banner confirms the appliance is operating standalone from internal storage. Every core feature — browsing the library, queueing tracks, transport, and volume control — works completely offline with zero internet access, zero app installation, and zero obligation to configure anything further.

Mobile data routing on phones: The appliance's network has no internet connection behind it. Modern phones (especially Android and iOS) may notice this and quietly send browser requests over cellular data instead, causing the page to fail to load. If that happens: temporarily turn off mobile/cellular data on your phone before connecting, or select the "Stay connected without internet" prompt in your phone's Wi-Fi settings.

Coming home: leaving the Lempi network

Once the appliance is running its own Lempi network, it gets back onto your home Wi-Fi in one of these ways. The speaker button and the automatic return both wait until no phone or laptop is connected to the Lempi network — while one is, someone is using it.

  • From the page: in the settings panel's Wi-Fi section, Stop, return to Wi-Fi joins the home network the appliance knows. Your browser loses the page as it switches; rejoin your home Wi-Fi, reload, and answer Yes, keep it on the “Keep this network?” banner. Unconfirmed after five minutes, the appliance goes back to its Lempi network, as any network switch does. If home can't be reached at all, the Lempi network comes straight back.
  • With a Bluetooth speaker connected — press previous-track on the speaker. Usually that speaker is also the battery, so the appliance doesn't try on its own: each try briefly disturbs the radio the music travels over. Press it once you're home, with no phone on the Lempi network. You'll hear a soft click as it starts, then within about fifteen seconds either two rising notes — it's back on your home Wi-Fi — or one low note: home didn't answer, and the Lempi network is back up. No click at all means the press was ignored: the appliance wasn't on its own network, a phone was still connected to it, or your speaker doesn't send that button. The music carries on throughout.
  • With no Bluetooth speaker connected (a wired speaker, so mains power): the appliance tries on its own, ninety seconds after the last device leaves the Lempi network, then less often after each failed try — three minutes, six, twelve, and every fifteen after that — so a week away isn't spent retrying.
  • Off and on again. Switching the appliance off and on — on a speaker that powers it, the speaker's own power switch — tries home first: it waits for known Wi-Fi before starting the Lempi network.

While the Lempi network is up, the appliance's log records each time a device joins or leaves it.

Moving to a new Wi-Fi network, or pivoting to a phone hotspot

From the same settings panel's Wi-Fi section (or by clicking Pivot to Hotspot / Wi-Fi on the offline banner), you can move the appliance onto a venue Wi-Fi network or a phone's personal hotspot:

  • Scanned networks: Click Scan for networks to discover nearby SSIDs, select one, and enter its password.
  • Single-phone hotspot (manual entry): A smartphone cannot broadcast a Wi-Fi hotspot while joined to Lempi's network at the same time. To connect Lempi to your phone's hotspot from that same phone, click Enter manually and type your phone hotspot's exact SSID and password before turning on your phone's hotspot. Click Connect, then turn on Personal Hotspot on your phone. Lempi will connect to your hotspot.

Finding Lempi on a hotspot: Once Lempi joins your phone's hotspot, you can reach it at http://<ip>/ (on iOS hotspots, Lempi is usually http://172.20.10.x; on Android, check the IP listed under Hotspot Connected Devices). The Lempi mobile app's network search looks for a Vipunen hub to join, not for players, so it will not find the appliance this way.

Confirm-or-revert safety & reload persistence: Switching networks temporarily severs your browser connection. Every switch schedules a safety timer (3–5 minutes). When you rejoin on the new network and reload the page, the "Keep this network?" banner is automatically restored with the live remaining countdown. Click Yes, keep it to disarm the timer. If unconfirmed or unreachable, the appliance automatically reverts to its previous working state (or back to Lempi AP mode), so it can never strand itself.

Play History (Lempi skin only)

The clock icon opens a page of everything that's actually played or been skipped, most recent first, with a page-size choice (10 / 100 / 1000 rows). Each row shows when, title and artist (both clickable into the preference panel), album, how much of it played, and whether it counted as a play or a skip. A checkbox per row lets you flag it for review — this doesn't change anything about playback; it puts the track on a worklist inside Vipunen's own console (the "Flags" page) so you or whoever curates the library later knows something about it seemed worth a second look. Once whatever needed a look has been dealt with, that same Flags page has its own unflag button on every row — no need to open the track's own profile page first — which clears it there and, if a remote is configured, on the remote too, in one click.

"Why this passage?" and the programme

Every skin shows at least a one-line summary of why the current track was picked. The Lempi skin alone shows the full breakdown: a table of every factor that adjusted the odds (how the exact math works is in the Appendix), and an "It beat" list of the other tracks that were in the running but lost, each clickable into its own preference panel. In the Lempi skin you can also click any queued (not-yet-playing) track to see its own explanation instead of the current track's.

A programme is this player's whole day of listening, under a name, such as "WKMP". It is made of time slots — "Mellow" from 22:00, "Fun" from 16:30 — each of which steers what kind of music plays from its start until the next one starts. The Lempi and WinAmp skins show the time slots in a dropdown, with "Automatic (by time of day)"; MuLibPlay shows a row of buttons and an "Autoselect by clock time" checkbox that does the same thing. Picking a time slot by hand holds it until you switch back to automatic; it takes effect on the next track chosen, not instantly. See the Appendix for what a time slot consists of.

Edit programme, beside that list, opens the programme this player runs: its name, and each time slot with its start, the range it is on air for, and its seeds — the recordings, shown by title, artist and album, that set its sound. You can rename the programme and its time slots, change when each starts, add or remove a time slot, and add or remove seeds — Add a seed… under a time slot searches the library by title or artist. A song you are hearing, or find in Browse, can be made a seed from its own panel too: tap its title (or the ☆ beside it in Browse) and tick the time slots under Seed of. The order seeds are listed in does not matter: for each choice the player takes one seed per artist, those played least recently, at most five. Save keeps the programme's name; Save as a new programme… asks for another, so the programme you started from stays as it was wherever else it is kept, and this player now runs the new one. Either way the change is this player's alone, and it takes effect on the next track chosen.

Restarting or shutting down

The settings panel's restart and shutdown buttons both ask for confirmation before acting. Shutdown is one-way from the web interface — nothing on this page can turn the appliance back on once it's off; you'll need physical access to it.

To shut down after the music rather than now, the MuLibPlay skin's Enqueue Shutdown button puts a Shutdown entry at the end of the queue. The track before it plays to its end, with no crossfade into anything after it; then the appliance powers off, and whatever was queued behind it is still there at the next power-on. The entry shows in the queue in every skin and can be removed like any queued track; there is only ever one.

Putting back an earlier copy of your listening (Lempi skin only)

Lempi copies your plays, preferences, likes and programmes aside every hour, and keeps one a day for a week, one a month for a year and one a year for good. The settings panel's Listening backups row lists them. What would this do? says how many plays a copy would put back, and changes nothing. Put this back at restart stages it: the copy goes back the next time Lempi starts, not while it is playing. Until then Cancel undoes the choice.

Just before putting a copy back, Lempi keeps the listening as it is at that moment. That copy appears in the same list, marked "kept from before a restore", so a wrong choice can be undone the same way, and nothing played since the copy you chose is lost: it is in that kept copy. The row then says what the restore did. The music library itself is not part of these copies.

Sending music to another Lempi

Vipunen's Export page has two ways to get music to a Lempi that doesn't have it yet:

  • Send what's missing to the speakers — for the household's own players. It asks each speaker you tick which audio it holds (nothing on it changes), and builds one bundle of everything this library has that it lacks. Send to that speaker shows what the send would do; Send now then copies it into the speaker's own music folder and imports it there.
  • Build a bundle for a remote Lempi — for any other installation. It bundles whatever matches your search (artist, album, title) plus everything Vipunen knows about it, and shows the commands to move it there; for this one, nothing runs on another machine until you run those commands yourself.

Keeping libraries in sync

The household's routine is Vipunen's Sync page: it takes each ticked player's edits — preferences, flags, likes, corrections — merges them on the desktop, asks you for a verdict wherever two of them disagree, and sends the result back out. Each player applies it itself, without stopping. Plays are never shared: each player keeps its own history.

For a one-off between two libraries, Vipunen's Flags page also has a "sync with a remote" section, once you've told it about another library (a host and file path):

  • Pull flags from remote — brings in anything flagged for review on the other system.
  • Push edits to remote — sends identification and boundary corrections you've applied locally to the other system, briefly interrupting its playback while it applies them (never automatic, always something you choose to do).
  • Sync preferences — reconciles your Preference Tuning dials (Cooldown/Recovery/Preference) between the two systems, for whichever artists/recordings both actually know about, the newer change winning on each side.
