Advanced Features

Everything below exists somewhere in Lempi or Vipunen today. Quite a lot of it — settings, Bluetooth, play history, the full "why did it pick this" breakdown — only exists in the Lempi skin; the table at the bottom of this page says exactly what's where.

Settings (Lempi skin only)

The gear icon opens a settings panel with a run of fields that shape playback behavior:

  • Skip fade — how long a manual skip fades out over, rather than cutting instantly.
  • Next starts at — how much lead-in time the next track gets before the current one actually ends.
  • Save position every… — how often the current playback position is written down, so a restart resumes close to where it left off.
  • Hold a skipped/removed track out for… — two separate windows: how long a track you skip, or a track you remove from the queue, is held back from being offered again. This is distinct from the Cooldown dial in Preference Tuning, and shorter.
  • Keep queued ahead — how many tracks Lempi tries to keep lined up in the queue at once.
  • Guest sampling interval — only relevant if another playback backend (like MPD) is attached alongside Lempi.
  • Playing through — which backend is actually driving audio right now, if more than one is available; hidden entirely when there's only the one.
  • Status LED — on a Raspberry Pi appliance, controls the small light on the device itself. Four choices: Always on (the default, with its own brightness slider), Live Wi-Fi indicator (the light reflects whether Wi-Fi is currently switched on — useful on an appliance, since it's the only sign of life if the network drops), Always off, or Leave to the system (Lempi doesn't manage it at all). Below full brightness, "Always on" blinks very fast to look dimmer rather than glowing at reduced power — this LED has no true hardware dimmer. Has no effect on playback either way, and the choice is remembered and reapplied automatically at every restart, including a full power cycle.
  • Four file-writing toggles, each with its own on-screen explanation: naming capture tracks for other clients (cue sheets), saving album art for captures, keeping per-song lyrics for a local client, and keeping lyrics files beside the audio itself. Some of these only take effect after a restart — the panel says which.

The same panel also shows the exact build you're running (with a copy-to-clipboard button), which branch and commit it came from, and whether the working copy has uncommitted changes — useful when reporting a problem or checking whether an update actually landed.

Bluetooth and other radios (Lempi skin only)

The same settings panel lists every radio the device has (Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, mobile) with on/off switches — a radio carrying your only route to this page can't be switched off from here, for the obvious reason. The speaker chooser scans for, pairs with, connects to ("use this one"), or forgets a Bluetooth speaker. After connecting, a "can you hear it?" check gives you thirty seconds to confirm before automatically reverting if you don't — so a bad connection can't strand you silently.

Travel, offline playback, and automatic Wi-Fi failover (Lempi skin only, Raspberry Pi appliance only)

When you take a Pi appliance travelling or move it out of range of its home network, you don't need a screen, keyboard, or pre-configured router: it takes care of itself. If it boots where no known Wi-Fi is reachable (or loses connection for 30 continuous seconds), it automatically starts its own access point named Lempi (default password: Lempi321).

First-class offline playback: You can join that network from your phone, tablet, or laptop and reach the player directly at plain http://lempi/ or http://10.42.0.1/ — no port number needed (port 5720 works as well). An Offline Player banner confirms the appliance is operating standalone from internal storage. Every core feature — browsing the library, queueing tracks, transport, and volume control — works completely offline with zero internet access, zero app installation, and zero obligation to configure anything further.

Mobile data routing on phones: The appliance's network has no internet connection behind it. Modern phones (especially Android and iOS) may notice this and quietly send browser requests over cellular data instead, causing the page to fail to load. If that happens: temporarily turn off mobile/cellular data on your phone before connecting, or select the "Stay connected without internet" prompt in your phone's Wi-Fi settings.

Moving to a new Wi-Fi network, or pivoting to a phone hotspot

From the same settings panel's Wi-Fi section (or by clicking Pivot to Hotspot / Wi-Fi on the offline banner), you can move the appliance onto a venue Wi-Fi network or a phone's personal hotspot:

  • Scanned networks: Click Scan for networks to discover nearby SSIDs, select one, and enter its password.
  • Single-phone hotspot (manual entry): A smartphone cannot broadcast a Wi-Fi hotspot while joined to Lempi's network at the same time. To connect Lempi to your phone's hotspot from that same phone, click Enter manually and type your phone hotspot's exact SSID and password before turning on your phone's hotspot. Click Connect, then turn on Personal Hotspot on your phone. Lempi will connect to your hotspot.

Finding Lempi on a hotspot: Once Lempi joins your phone's hotspot, you can reach it at http://<ip>/ (on iOS hotspots, Lempi is usually http://172.20.10.x; on Android, check the IP listed under Hotspot Connected Devices) or by opening the Lempi mobile app, which finds the player automatically via local network broadcast.

Confirm-or-revert safety & reload persistence: Switching networks temporarily severs your browser connection. Every switch schedules a safety timer (3–5 minutes). When you rejoin on the new network and reload the page, the "Keep this network?" banner is automatically restored with the live remaining countdown. Click Yes, keep it to disarm the timer. If unconfirmed or unreachable, the appliance automatically reverts to its previous working state (or back to Lempi AP mode), so it can never strand itself.

Play History (Lempi skin only)

The clock icon opens a page of everything that's actually played or been skipped, most recent first, with a page-size choice (10 / 100 / 1000 rows). Each row shows when, title and artist (both clickable into the preference panel), album, how much of it played, and whether it counted as a play or a skip. A checkbox per row lets you flag it for review — this doesn't change anything about playback; it puts the track on a worklist inside Vipunen's own console (the "Flags" page) so you or whoever curates the library later knows something about it seemed worth a second look. Once whatever needed a look has been dealt with, that same Flags page has its own unflag button on every row — no need to open the track's own profile page first — which clears it there and, if a remote is configured, on the remote too, in one click.

"Why this passage?" and program schedules

Every skin shows at least a one-line summary of why the current track was picked. The Lempi skin alone shows the full breakdown: a table of every factor that adjusted the odds (how the exact math works is in the Appendix), and an "It beat" list of the other tracks that were in the running but lost, each clickable into its own preference panel. In the Lempi skin you can also click any queued (not-yet-playing) track to see its own explanation instead of the current track's.

A program is a named block that steers what kind of music plays at a given time of day — the Lempi and WinAmp skins show a dropdown of the available programs plus "Automatic (by time of day)"; MuLibPlay instead shows a row of program buttons and an "Autoselect by clock time" checkbox that does the same thing. Picking a program by hand overrides the schedule until you switch back to automatic; it takes effect on the next track chosen, not instantly. There is currently no way to create a new program or change what one sounds like from the web interface — you can only choose among whatever programs already exist. See the Appendix for what a program actually consists of.

Restarting or shutting down

The settings panel's restart and shutdown buttons both ask for confirmation before acting. Shutdown is one-way from the web interface — nothing on this page can turn the appliance back on once it's off; you'll need physical access to it.

Sending music to another Lempi

Vipunen's Export page bundles up matching tracks (search by artist/album/title) plus everything Vipunen knows about them into a folder, for a Lempi installation that doesn't have that music yet. It doesn't copy your whole library — just what matches — and it shows you the exact commands to actually move that bundle to the other machine and tell it to notice the new music; it doesn't run those commands for you.

Keeping two libraries in sync

Vipunen's Flags page also has a "sync with a remote" section, once you've told it about another library (a host and file path):

  • Pull flags from remote — brings in anything flagged for review on the other system.
  • Push edits to remote — sends identification and boundary corrections you've applied locally to the other system, briefly interrupting its playback while it applies them (never automatic, always something you choose to do).
  • Sync preferences — reconciles your Preference Tuning dials (Cooldown/Recovery/Preference) between the two systems, for whichever artists/recordings both actually know about, the newer change winning on each side.
